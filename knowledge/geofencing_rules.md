# The India EEZ and the IMBL

India's Exclusive Economic Zone extends 200 nautical miles from the baseline.
Inside it India has rights over fisheries and other resources. Fishing beyond it,
or across an International Maritime Boundary Line (IMBL) into another country's
waters, exposes the crew to arrest and the boat to confiscation.

The highest-risk stretch for Indian fishermen is Palk Bay and the Gulf of Mannar,
where the India-Sri Lanka IMBL runs close to traditional grounds and the water is
shallow on both sides. Arrests by the Sri Lanka Navy are common. Kachchhativu
island lies on the Sri Lankan side of the line. The practical rule used by
fisheries departments is to turn back well before the line rather than navigate
to it, because GPS error, current set and the absence of any visible marker make
the boundary easy to overrun.

The northern Bay of Bengal boundary with Bangladesh was delimited by an UNCLOS
Annex VII arbitral award in 2014. The Gujarat coast faces the unresolved Sir
Creek area with Pakistan, where the risk of arrest is high and the boundary is
disputed, not merely unmarked.

# Marine protected areas

Fishing, anchoring and collection are restricted or prohibited in notified marine
protected areas. The main ones on the Indian coast:

- Gulf of Mannar Marine National Park, Tamil Nadu. A chain of 21 islands with
  coral reefs and seagrass. Core zone closed to fishing.
- Marine National Park and Sanctuary, Gulf of Kachchh, Gujarat. India's first
  marine national park.
- Gahirmatha Marine Sanctuary, Odisha, within Bhitarkanika. The world's largest
  olive ridley turtle nesting beach. A seasonal fishing ban applies during the
  nesting period and turtle excluder devices are mandatory in the surrounding
  waters.
- Malvan Marine Sanctuary, Maharashtra. Trawling prohibited.
- Mahatma Gandhi Marine National Park, Wandoor, Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
- Sundarbans, West Bengal. Biosphere reserve and tiger reserve; entry permits and
  channel restrictions apply.

# Other restricted waters

- Launch hazard areas around SDSC Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh. Closed when a
  NAVAREA warning is in force for a launch. Always check the current NAVAREA
  VIII notice.
- Naval exercise areas and firing ranges, promulgated through NAVAREA and local
  notices to mariners.
- Port limits, approach channels and fairways, where fishing and anchoring are
  restricted for traffic safety.
- Offshore oil and gas fields, notably Bombay High, with 500 m safety zones
  around every installation.

# Seasonal closures

Every maritime state enforces an annual monsoon fishing ban on mechanised
vessels, generally 61 days: roughly mid-April to mid-June on the east coast and
June to July on the west coast. Exact dates are notified by the state fisheries
department each year and differ between states, so the state notification is the
authority, not a general rule.

# Gear and zone rules

Most states reserve inshore waters, typically within 3 to 12 nautical miles
depending on the state, for traditional and non-mechanised craft. Trawling inside
that limit is an offence under the state Marine Fishing Regulation Act. Pair
trawling, bull trawling and the use of LED lights to attract fish are banned in
several states.
