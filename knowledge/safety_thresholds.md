# Wave height bands used for small craft

Significant wave height (SWH) is the average height of the highest one third of
waves. Individual waves can be up to twice the SWH, and that is what capsizes
small boats.

- Below 1.0 m: smooth to slight. Normal operations for traditional craft.
- 1.0 to 1.5 m: slight to moderate. Safe for most motorised boats, uncomfortable
  for small country craft.
- 1.5 to 2.5 m: moderate to rough. INCOIS routinely advises caution in this band.
  Small craft and catamarans should stay in. Fibre boats under 10 m should not
  venture beyond the surf zone.
- 2.5 to 4.0 m: rough to very rough. Fishing operations should be suspended.
  This is the band at which INCOIS issues high wave alerts for the coast.
- Above 4.0 m: very rough to high. All craft should remain in harbour.

# Wind speed bands

- Below 10 kt (Beaufort 3): light. No restriction.
- 10 to 17 kt (Beaufort 4 to 5): moderate to fresh breeze. Sea becomes choppy.
- 17 to 27 kt (Beaufort 5 to 6): fresh to strong breeze. IMD language for this is
  usually "squally weather". Small craft advisory conditions.
- 27 to 34 kt (Beaufort 7): near gale. Fishermen are advised not to venture.
- Above 34 kt (Beaufort 8 and above): gale. Harbour warning conditions. Boats
  already at sea should return immediately or run for the nearest shelter.

Gusts matter more than mean wind for a small boat. A mean of 20 kt gusting 35 kt
is more dangerous than a steady 25 kt because the boat cannot settle.

# Surface currents

Surface currents above about 50 cm/s (roughly 1 knot) make it hard for small
craft to hold position while fishing, and they significantly change fuel
planning on the return leg. Currents above 100 cm/s combined with opposing wind
produce a short steep sea that is dangerous out of proportion to the wave height.

# Combined judgement

The dangerous combinations, in order of how often they cause accidents:

1. Wind opposing swell. A moderate SWH with wind from the opposite quarter
   produces breaking seas.
2. Short wave period with moderate height. An SWH of 2 m at a 5 second period is
   far worse than 2 m at 12 seconds, because the waves are steep and close.
3. Rapidly rising wind after a calm morning, which is the classic pre-monsoon
   and thunderstorm pattern along the east coast.
4. Poor visibility with traffic, near harbour approaches and fairways.
