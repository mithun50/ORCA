# What a PFZ advisory is

INCOIS issues Potential Fishing Zone (PFZ) advisories for the Indian coast using
satellite sea surface temperature and ocean colour (chlorophyll-a). The advisory
names locations where fish are likely to aggregate, given as a distance and
bearing from a named landing centre, along with the depth contour.

The service began at NRSA in 1996-97 using NOAA-AVHRR sea surface temperature.
It now combines SST from Indian and international sensors with chlorophyll from
ocean colour monitors including Oceansat-2 OCM and EOS-06 (Oceansat-3) OCM-3.
Advisories are issued on working days for each maritime state, subject to cloud
cover, and are disseminated through state fisheries departments, electronic
display boards at landing centres, radio, and mobile services.

A PFZ advisory is a likelihood statement, not a guarantee. Cloud cover over the
Bay of Bengal during the monsoon frequently prevents issue for days at a time.

# The physical basis

Fish aggregate where food aggregates. Two satellite-visible signatures matter:

- **Thermal fronts.** A sharp horizontal gradient in sea surface temperature
  marks the meeting of two water masses. Plankton concentrates along the front,
  and so do the fish that feed on it. Gradients of roughly 0.5 degC over 10 km
  or more are considered significant along the Indian coast.
- **Chlorophyll fronts and patches.** Chlorophyll-a is a proxy for phytoplankton
  biomass, the base of the food chain. Values above roughly 0.5 mg/m3 in
  otherwise oligotrophic water indicate enrichment; coastal upwelling zones off
  Kerala and Karnataka in the southwest monsoon can exceed 3 mg/m3.

The most productive signature is where a thermal front and a chlorophyll front
coincide. Warm-core and cold-core eddies also concentrate productivity at their
edges rather than their centres.

# Favourable ranges for common Indian fisheries

- Oil sardine and Indian mackerel: sea surface temperature about 27 to 29 degC
  with chlorophyll above 0.5 mg/m3. Strongly associated with the southwest
  monsoon upwelling along the Kerala and Karnataka coast.
- Tuna (skipjack, yellowfin): 27 to 30 degC, often at the edges of thermal
  fronts and along the 200 m depth contour and beyond.
- Seer fish and carangids: 26 to 29 degC near fronts over the shelf.
- Shrimp trawl grounds: driven more by bottom sediment and river discharge than
  by SST.

Sea surface temperature above about 31 degC tends to suppress catch in surface
waters because the mixed layer becomes shallow and stratified, and the
nutrient supply from below is cut off.

# How to read an advisory safely

A PFZ advisory says nothing about whether it is safe to reach the location. It
must always be combined with the Ocean State Forecast and any IMD warning in
force. A productive zone 60 km offshore in a 3 m sea is not a fishing
opportunity.
